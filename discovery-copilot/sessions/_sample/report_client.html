AI & Automation Audit

Hartwell Home Furnishings

July 1, 2026  ·  Prepared for Dana by MK Operating Company

What we heard

  • "Everything lives on paper and in my head, and I'm the bottleneck for all of it" — the delivery calendar, the special-orders binder, the whiteboard, the sticky notes.
  • Missed inquiries are costing real sales: a Facebook message that sat two weeks lost a $2,000 dining set, and missed calls hit an answering machine whose tape is usually full.
  • About 1 in 5 deliveries bounce because reminder calls only happen when Dana remembers at 8pm — and Marcus bills for the truck either way.
  • The books are three months behind in QuickBooks Desktop 2019, Square sales get keyed in from printed reports, and tax season has meant extensions plus an $1,100 cleanup bill two years running.
  • Dana works ~65 hours a week but only about half of it is selling: "I'm the best closer here and I spend half my week being a very bad office administrator."

Recommendations

Automation Systems

Systems that respond, follow up, and remind — automatically.

Missed-Call Text-Back

Quick win
The problem

When the phone rings out, callers hit a full answering machine and "just call the next store." Dana knows sales are leaking but can't say how many.

What it does

When a call to the store goes unanswered, the caller instantly gets a text: "Hi, it's Hartwell Home Furnishings — sorry we missed you! How can we help?" Replies land in one shared inbox that Dana and Ellie can answer between customers.

What changes for you

You stop losing the callers who won't leave a voicemail. Every missed call becomes a conversation you can pick up when you're free instead of a customer at Ashley.

Delivery Scheduling + Reminder Texts

Standard build
The problem

Two trucks run off a paper calendar plus Marcus's notes app — this morning both trucks hit the same house while the Hendersons got nothing. ~1 in 5 deliveries bounce because nobody was home.

What it does

One shared delivery calendar that Dana, Ellie, and Marcus all see. Customers automatically get a confirmation text when booked and a reminder the day before with a reply-to-confirm; no answer flags the stop for a quick call.

What changes for you

One source of truth instead of three. Bounced trips drop sharply — and Marcus's billed truck time goes to deliveries that actually complete.

Order-Status Text Updates

Standard build
The problem

"'Where's my order' is probably a third of the phone traffic some weeks" — each answer takes ~20 minutes of digging through the special-orders binder.

What it does

Custom orders go into a simple tracker instead of (only) the binder. When the status changes — ordered, at the factory, shipped, arrived — the customer gets a short text automatically. A 'check my order' link shows them the same status any time.

What changes for you

The binder archaeology stops. A third of your inbound calls disappear, and customers feel looked after during the 6–10 week wait instead of forgotten.

Review Requests After Delivery

Quick win
The problem

~40 Google reviews (with two old delivery complaints on top) vs. ~900 at Ashley up the road — because asking felt tacky, so nobody ever asks.

What it does

The day after a completed delivery, the customer gets a text thanking them and asking how it went, with a link to leave a Google review. Unhappy replies come to you privately first instead of going public.

What changes for you

Review count starts compounding with zero asking-in-person awkwardness, and the two old complaints get buried under fresh five-stars.

Back-Office Services

Work we take off your plate and run for you.

Books Cleanup + Monthly Close

Larger project
The problem

QuickBooks Desktop 2019 is ~3 months behind, Square sales are keyed in from printed reports, and the accountant charges an $1,100 'shoebox surcharge' every spring after two straight extension years.

What it does

One-time cleanup and migration to QuickBooks Online with the Square feed connected, then a done-for-you monthly close: reconciled accounts and a plain-English P&L in your inbox by the 10th.

What changes for you

You stop running the store on "vibes and the bank balance." Pam stops retyping printed reports, tax season stops costing extensions and cleanup fees, and you finally see whether last month made money while it still matters.

Balance Collection on Autopilot

Standard build
The problem

Final balances are collected by memory — "a couple thousand floating out there on a sticky note" — and the biggest account pays in 60–90 days because chasing Brightview is the call Dana hates most.

What it does

Every delivery balance gets a text with a pay-by-card link the morning of delivery; unpaid balances get two automatic friendly nudges. Account invoices (the decorators, Brightview) get polite scheduled reminders at 30/45/60 days — from the system, not from Dana.

What changes for you

The sticky note retires. Money shows up without you having to be the bad guy, and the awkward Brightview call becomes an automatic email that isn't personal.

Custom Builds

Purpose-built tools for how your business actually works.

Past-Customer Reactivation (31 Years of Files)

Larger project
The problem

Three decades of customers sit in a filing cabinet and Square history, never contacted once — in an industry where people replace furniture every 7–10 years.

What it does

We pull what's already digital (Square customer list), digitize the recent paper tickets worth having, and build one clean customer list. Then a simple, occasional touch: a seasonal postcard-style email or text — new arrivals, a private sale for past customers — replacing the unused Constant Contact.

What changes for you

The 2018 bedroom-set buyers hear from you right when they're due again. Slow weeks get a lever you can actually pull, funded by cancelling ~$164/month of tools doing nothing.
